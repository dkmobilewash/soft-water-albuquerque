Where the water comes from

City wells west of downtown

The City of Belen supplies its customers with groundwater. Most of the city's wells sit on the west side of town, out near I-25, rather than among the older blocks around the rail yard. From there, the distribution system carries water to homes around Becker Avenue, the Harvey House Museum and beyond.

In 2022 the city finished an arsenic treatment plant. Arsenic is a naturally occurring element in parts of the Middle Rio Grande's groundwater, and public systems have to treat it to the federal limit. If you'd like the official figures, the city's annual Consumer Confidence Report lists what was tested and what was found.

What city treatment leaves in

Removing arsenic isn't the same as softening. Calcium and magnesium aren't health contaminants, so no utility is required to take them out, and groundwater in the region commonly tests hard. That hardness is what builds up on Belen faucets, coffee makers and water heater elements.

Matching the fix to the problem

Most Belen homes on city water start with hardness. A water softener for your Belen home exchanges calcium and magnesium at the main line, which protects tank and tankless heaters alike and takes the edge off dry skin in this very dry climate. It's the foundation of everything else we install.

If the complaint is taste or smell rather than scale, catalytic carbon is the next layer. We combine it with the softener in a single tank, so there's no second unit crowding the garage. Our page on whole-home filtration in Belen covers what carbon reduces and where it stops.

For drinking and cooking water, an under-sink system is the right tool. Reverse osmosis in Belen lowers dissolved solids and is the standard point-of-use approach for arsenic and nitrate, which matters most to families pumping their own water outside city limits.

Belen water FAQs

Has Belen's 2022 arsenic plant changed what I need at home?

The plant addresses arsenic for city customers so the water meets the federal standard. It doesn't soften water, so scale and spotting are still the main reasons people in town look at treatment.

I'm across the river or out in the county. Do you still come out?

Yes. We test and install throughout the Belen area, including rural properties on private wells.

Who's responsible for testing a private well?

The owner. Private wells aren't regulated like public systems, so there's no annual report. We'll help you arrange lab testing so you know what you're working with.

Does the free test cost anything later?

No. It's free, it takes about fifteen minutes, and you keep the printed report whether or not you buy anything.
