Why hardness survives treatment

City of Belen water starts in wells on the west side of town near I-25. Every public supplier treats for what's regulated, and in Belen that now includes a dedicated arsenic plant. Calcium and magnesium sit outside those rules entirely. They're harmless to drink, which is exactly why they're left in, and they're the minerals behind the white ring on your sink and the scale inside your water heater.

Regional groundwater commonly tests hard, but each home is a little different, so we never quote a number before we've tested. Your results will show where your water lands, and that reading sets the size of the resin bed and how often the metered valve regenerates.

The limits of a softener

Ion exchange removes hardness and can handle small amounts of iron. It does not remove arsenic, nitrate, chlorine or bacteria. For those, you'd look at kitchen-sink RO, carbon filtration, or, for bacteria in a well, disinfection after a lab test.

On the test sheet

The on-site test takes about fifteen minutes and checks hardness, total dissolved solids, pH, chlorine, iron, manganese and sulfur. Iron matters most on private wells around the edges of Belen; it can stain fixtures orange and, at higher levels, needs more than a softener. If you're on a well, we'll also help you get lab testing for bacteria, nitrate and arsenic.

Questions about softeners in Belen

Does Belen's arsenic plant soften the water?

No. Arsenic treatment and softening are separate processes. The plant handles arsenic for city customers; hardness is left for homeowners to address if they choose.

Can you install in an older house near downtown Belen?

Yes. Older homes often lack a pre-plumbed softener loop, so we look at the main line during the test and quote any extra plumbing before you commit.

Will a softener help with orange stains from my well?

Light iron, yes: the resin captures a little of it along with the hardness. If your test shows more than that, we'll talk about specialty media rather than overloading the resin.

How much salt will I use?

It depends on your hardness and household size. The metered valve regenerates only after you've used enough water to need it, which keeps salt use down.
