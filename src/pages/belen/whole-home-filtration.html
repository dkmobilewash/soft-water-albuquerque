The short version for Belen

The city pumps groundwater from wells near I-25 and, since 2022, runs it through an arsenic treatment plant. Public water also carries a disinfectant to keep it safe on the way to your house, and that's often the taste and smell people want gone. Carbon is very good at that job. It is not an arsenic, nitrate or hardness treatment.

We hear about taste far more often than about anything else in town. Someone moves into a Belen house, fills a glass, and decides to keep buying bottled water. Filtration at the point where water enters the home changes that for drinking, cooking, bathing and laundry all at once, without a pitcher in the fridge or a filter on every faucet.

Before we recommend it, the free on-site test measures chlorine alongside hardness, TDS, pH, iron, manganese and sulfur. If the chlorine reading is low and taste isn't bothering you, we'll say so, and a softener alone may be all you need.

Belen filtration questions

Since Belen built an arsenic plant, why would I want a filter?

The plant handles arsenic. Carbon filtration is about taste, odor, sediment and VOCs, which are separate concerns. Many households want both a compliant supply and water they enjoy drinking.

My rural well smells like rotten eggs. Is carbon enough?

That smell usually points to sulfur, which may need specialty media. We test for it on-site and only recommend media your results support. We also suggest a lab test for bacteria, nitrate and arsenic.

Do I need two separate tanks for softening and filtering?

No. Our Soft + Filtered Home system does both in a single tank, which saves floor space in the garage or closet.
