Why hardness is so common in Bosque Farms

Every drop that comes out of a Bosque Farms faucet started as groundwater, whether it was pumped by the village or by your own well. Groundwater in this part of the Rio Grande valley commonly tests hard, carrying dissolved calcium and magnesium that drop out as scale when water heats up or evaporates. You see it as white rings on the stock tank, crust on the shower glass and a sputtering dishwasher.

A softener with ion-exchange resin captures those minerals and swaps them out, so the water entering your plumbing stops leaving deposits. It can also handle small amounts of iron, which matters on some private wells.

What a softener won't do

Softening does not remove arsenic, nitrate, chlorine or bacteria. Naturally occurring arsenic has been an ongoing issue the village is working to address, and the village's annual water quality report is where to find official results. If arsenic is on your mind, the right tool is reverse osmosis at the kitchen sink, not a bigger softener.

Testing before sizing

Our free on-site test reads hardness, TDS, pH, chlorine, iron and manganese, and sulfur in about 15 minutes. Hardness and household size decide the resin capacity and how often the metered valve regenerates. Iron and manganese tell us whether the resin needs help. Your results will show where your water lands; we won't size a system off a guess. Private well owners also get our help lining up lab testing for arsenic, nitrate and bacteria.

Softener questions from Bosque Farms

Is Bosque Farms village water hard?

Groundwater in the area commonly tests hard. Hardness varies, so our free test gives you a reading for your own tap, and the village's annual water quality report covers the system as a whole.

If I soften my water, is it safe to drink?

A softener isn't a drinking-water treatment. It changes hardness, not arsenic, nitrate or bacteria. Village water is tested and reported annually; for a private well, lab testing is the way to answer safety questions.

My well water leaves orange stains. Can a softener handle it?

Small amounts of iron, yes. Heavier iron or manganese may need specialty media ahead of the softener. The test tells us which.

Where does the brine discharge go?

Into a proper drain connection inside the home, set up during the install so it's code-compliant.
