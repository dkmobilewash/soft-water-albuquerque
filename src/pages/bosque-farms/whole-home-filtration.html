The village disinfects its deep-well water with chlorine, which is exactly what a public system should do. Catalytic carbon at your main line reduces that taste and smell once the water is in your house — and on a private well, the right media can deal with sediment, iron or sulfur.

What filtration helps with in Bosque Farms

And, just as important, what it doesn't.

Chlorine

Reduces the pool-water taste and odor that village disinfection can leave behind, in showers as well as drinking glasses.

Sediment

Catches fine grit that can come up with well water and wear on valves, aerators and appliances.

Many VOCs

Catalytic carbon reduces a range of volatile organic compounds along with off tastes.

Well-specific media

Iron, manganese or sulfur on a private well can be addressed with specialty media once a test confirms them.

Carbon is not an arsenic treatment

Whole-home carbon does not remove arsenic, nitrate or hardness. Arsenic in local groundwater is something the village is working to address, and its annual water quality report has the official data. For drinking water at home, under-sink reverse osmosis is the usual approach.

Filtration FAQs for Bosque Farms

Does Bosque Farms use chlorine in its water?

Yes. The village disinfects its well water with chlorine, which keeps it safe in the distribution pipes. Carbon filtration simply reduces the taste and smell once it reaches your home.

Will a whole-home filter lower arsenic?

No. Catalytic carbon isn't designed for arsenic. Point-of-use reverse osmosis is the standard home approach, and performance should be confirmed with testing.

My well smells like rotten eggs. What helps?

That odor usually points to sulfur. Depending on the test, catalytic carbon or a specialty media may reduce it. We confirm with a sulfur reading first.

Do I need a softener too?

If your hardness reading is high, pairing the two makes sense, and our combined tank keeps the footprint small. If hardness isn't an issue, we'll say so.
