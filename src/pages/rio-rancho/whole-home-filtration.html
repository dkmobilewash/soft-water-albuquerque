On city water: it's about taste and the whole-house feel

The city disinfects its well water before it heads into the mains, and a residual stays in the pipes all the way to your house. That's exactly what's supposed to happen. Some people just don't like the smell in a hot shower or the taste in a pot of coffee. A catalytic carbon bed reduces that, along with sediment and many volatile organic compounds, at every faucet instead of only the kitchen.

Well owners: test before you filter

A rotten-egg smell or orange staining from a Rio Rancho Estates well might be sulfur or iron, and specialty media can sometimes handle those. But we won't guess. Carbon doesn't remove arsenic or nitrate, and nothing we install treats bacteria. We help you get lab results first, then size equipment to them.

On a well, the filter tank goes after the pressure tank, typically in the garage or a utility room, so the pressure switch and tank keep working as they always have.

Filtration FAQs for Rio Rancho

Will a whole-home filter remove the chlorine smell in my Rio Rancho shower?

Catalytic carbon is designed to reduce chlorine taste and odor, and because it treats water where it enters the house, the shower benefits along with the kitchen.

Do I need filtration if I already have a softener?

They do different jobs. The softener handles scale. Carbon handles taste, odor, sediment and many VOCs. If taste isn't bothering you, a softener alone may be enough.

Will carbon fix my well water in Rio Rancho Estates?

It depends on what's in it. Carbon helps with sediment and some odors, and specialty media may handle iron, manganese or sulfur. It won't remove arsenic or nitrate, and bacteria needs disinfection, so lab testing comes first.

How long does a filtration install take?

About half a day, done by our own installers. The combined softener-and-carbon tank installs much like a softener on its own.
