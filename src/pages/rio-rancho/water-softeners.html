Where the hardness comes from

Unlike Albuquerque, which blends treated river water into its supply, Rio Rancho's city utility relies on wells. As water sits in and moves through the aquifer beneath the west mesa, it dissolves calcium and magnesium from the sediment around it. By the time it reaches your house, it's usually somewhere in the moderately hard to hard range. That's not a safety issue, and the city's annual water quality report confirms the system meets federal standards. It's a scale issue.

Scale is what you see as white rings on the shower door in Cabezon, crusty aerators in Vista Hills, or a dishwasher in Northern Meadows that leaves every glass cloudy. Inside the walls, the same minerals coat water heater elements and the heat exchanger of a tankless unit, which costs efficiency every single day.

What a softener does, and doesn't do

An ion-exchange softener swaps hardness minerals for sodium (or potassium, if you prefer) as water passes through a resin bed. It can also handle small amounts of iron. It does not remove arsenic, nitrate, chlorine or bacteria, so if those are your concern, we'll talk about reverse osmosis or carbon filtration instead of overselling a softener.

If you're on a well in Rio Rancho Estates

Private well water out on the bigger lots can run harder or softer than city water, and sometimes carries iron. Our test checks hardness, TDS, pH, iron and manganese, sulfur and chlorine right at your sink, so we size the resin bed for the water you actually have. Because wells aren't regulated like the city system, we also help you set up lab testing for bacteria, nitrate and arsenic, which no softener addresses.

Softener questions from Rio Rancho homeowners

Will a softener stop the spots on my shower glass in Rio Rancho?

Hardness minerals cause most of those spots, so yes, softened water dramatically cuts new buildup. Existing scale needs one good cleaning, and after that it stays far easier to keep clear.

My Rio Rancho house has a tankless water heater. Does that change anything?

It makes a softener more worthwhile. Tankless units push water through a narrow heat exchanger, and scale there hurts performance quickly. Softening upstream keeps that exchanger clean.

Is 48,000 grains big enough for my house?

For most Rio Rancho households on city water, yes. Larger families or harder well water may need more capacity, and the free test tells us which.

Can a softener fix iron staining from my Rio Rancho Estates well?

It can handle small amounts of iron. If testing shows more than that, we'll explain the options rather than promise a softener will cover it.
