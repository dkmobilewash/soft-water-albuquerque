Two water stories, one city

City water and well water are different problems

Most Rio Rancho homes are connected to the city-owned utility, which pumps groundwater from its own wells, disinfects it and delivers it through the municipal system. The city publishes an annual water quality report (the Consumer Confidence Report), and that report shows the system meeting federal standards. If you want official numbers, that report is the place to start.

Meeting standards doesn't make water soft, though. Rio Rancho's groundwater is generally described as moderately hard to hard, and that's what most homeowners actually notice: chalky film on shower glass, crust around faucet aerators, and a water heater that starts popping and ticking long before it should.

Out in Rio Rancho Estates

Drive north and west past the subdivisions and the picture changes. Many lots in Rio Rancho Estates and other outlying areas rely on private domestic wells. No utility tests that water for you. The owner is responsible, and what comes out of one well can look quite different from the well a mile away.

For well owners, we run the same on-site test and then help arrange lab testing for things a sink-side test can't confirm, like bacteria, nitrate and arsenic. Treatment comes after the facts, not before.

Dry air, hard water

Why softening is so popular up on the mesa

The air on the west mesa is dry most of the year. Pair that with hard water and you get tight, itchy skin after a shower and hair that never quite rinses clean. Soft water won't change the humidity, but plenty of people say their skin feels noticeably better once the minerals are gone.

The bigger financial reason is the water heater. If you have a tankless heater, it's especially sensitive to scale on their heat exchangers. A softener protects them for the life of the unit.

Rio Rancho questions

How hard is Rio Rancho city water?

The city's groundwater is generally described as moderately hard to hard, but it varies with which wells are feeding your area. Our free test shows where your home lands, and the city's annual water quality report has the official figures.

I'm on a well in Rio Rancho Estates. Where do I start?

Start with testing. We do the on-site test for hardness, iron, sulfur and the rest, and help you arrange lab testing for bacteria, nitrate and arsenic. Once results are in, we'll explain which equipment, if any, makes sense.

Does a softener make city water safer to drink?

No. A softener deals with hardness minerals. It doesn't remove things like arsenic or nitrate. If you want better drinking water, reverse osmosis at the kitchen sink is the tool for that.

Do you install in the garage?

In many Rio Rancho homes the main water line and water heater are in the garage, so that's usually where the softener goes. We'll confirm the best spot during the test visit.
