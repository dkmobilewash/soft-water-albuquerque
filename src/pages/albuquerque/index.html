Where Albuquerque's tap water comes from

Most homes in the city are served by the Albuquerque Bernalillo County Water Utility Authority, usually shortened to ABCWUA or simply "the Water Authority." Its supply isn't a single source. Part of it is San Juan–Chama water carried into the Rio Grande and cleaned up at the utility's surface water treatment plant; the rest is groundwater pumped from the aquifer beneath the basin.

That blend matters at your faucet. In drought years the utility leans harder on groundwater, so the character of the water arriving at your house can drift from season to season and year to year. Owners often notice it first as heavier spotting on shower glass or a crust that seems to come back faster on the kettle.

For disinfection, the Water Authority uses chlorine, generated on-site as sodium hypochlorite. It's what keeps the distribution system safe, and it's also the reason some households notice a pool-like smell from a freshly drawn glass.

Four parts of town, four different starting points

Water from the same utility, but very different houses receiving it.

Nob Hill & the University area

Older homes near Central and UNM often have tight utility closets and decades of scale already inside the lines. Planning the install around limited space is half the job here.

Westside & Northwest Mesa

Taylor Ranch, Paradise Hills and Ventana Ranch are mostly newer subdivisions with garage water heaters and plenty of tankless units, which are worth protecting from scale early.

Foothills, NE Heights & Four Hills

Larger households, more bathrooms and lots of glass shower enclosures. Spotting and fixture buildup tend to be the complaints we hear most on the east side of town.

North & South Valley

Mostly on city water, but the South Valley still has homes on private wells while ABCWUA extends lines into well-dependent areas. A well calls for lab testing, not guesswork.

Albuquerque questions

Does Albuquerque use chlorine or chloramine?

ABCWUA disinfects with chlorine, which it produces on-site as sodium hypochlorite. That's good news for filtration, because catalytic carbon handles chlorine taste and odor very effectively.

Why does my water seem different some years?

The Water Authority blends treated surface water with groundwater, and the mix shifts toward groundwater in drought years. As the blend changes, so can the mineral character you notice on fixtures and glass.

Do you work in every part of the city?

Yes. We test and install across Albuquerque, from Downtown and Old Town to Uptown, the Academy area, the Westside, the Foothills and both valleys.

My South Valley home is on a well. Can you still help?

Yes. Private wells aren't regulated like the city system, so we start with our on-site test and help you arrange lab testing for bacteria, nitrate, arsenic and other concerns before recommending any equipment.
