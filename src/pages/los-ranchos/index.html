One village, two very different water stories

Drive Rio Grande Boulevard or Fourth Street through Los Ranchos and you pass newer homes on village water right next to adobe houses, barns and irrigated pasture that have relied on their own wells since long before the water lines arrived. The village identifies the Albuquerque Bernalillo County Water Utility Authority (ABCWUA) as its water provider, yet plenty of older valley properties never switched over. That split matters, because the right treatment for one household can be the wrong one for its neighbor.

Homes on ABCWUA water receive a blend of treated San Juan–Chama surface water and aquifer groundwater, disinfected with chlorine. The utility tests that water and publishes the results each year in its Consumer Confidence Report. What reaches your faucet is reliably treated, but it often carries enough mineral content to scale fixtures and enough chlorine to notice in a glass or a shower.

A private well is a different arrangement entirely. Nobody tests it for you. The homeowner is responsible for knowing what's in it, and shallow valley groundwater sits close to irrigation ditches, pastures and septic systems. That doesn't mean something is wrong with your well; it means the only way to know is to test it.

Not sure which supply your property uses?

Look for an ABCWUA bill, a water meter near the street, or a pressure tank in a well house, garage or closet. If you still can't tell, our technician will sort it out during the visit.

Questions from Los Ranchos homeowners

My neighbor is on ABCWUA water but I have a well. Do we need the same system?

Probably not. Utility water has already been disinfected and tested, so the usual concerns are hardness and chlorine taste. A well can carry iron, sulfur or other dissolved items, and it may need lab testing before anyone recommends equipment. We test each home on its own terms.

Will a softener interfere with watering my orchard or horses?

Irrigation from the ditch doesn't touch your household plumbing at all. For hose bibs and barn lines fed by the house, we can often plumb the softener so outdoor taps stay on untreated water, depending on how your lines are laid out.

Where does the equipment go on an older adobe home?

Wherever there's a cold-water main, a drain and an outlet nearby — often a garage, utility room or well house. On well systems it goes after the pressure tank. We look at your layout before quoting.

Can you tell me if my well water is safe to drink?

Our on-site test checks aesthetic and treatment-related items. Safety questions such as bacteria, nitrate and arsenic need a certified lab, and we'll help you arrange that testing.
