Common complaints

What filtration can help with east of the Sandias

These are the issues that tend to bring Tijeras and Cedar Crest homeowners to us. We confirm each one with a test before recommending anything.

🏊

Chlorine taste or smell

Community systems that disinfect can leave a residual you notice in a glass or the shower. Catalytic carbon reduces that taste and odor throughout the house.

🪨

Sediment and grit

Some wells push fine particles into the plumbing, clogging aerators and wearing on valves. Filtration catches sediment before it travels.

🥚

A rotten-egg odor

Some mountain wells carry a sulfur smell. If the on-site test confirms sulfur, specialty media can be added to address it.

Carbon filtration also reduces many VOCs. It does not remove hardness, arsenic or nitrate.

Well or community system: how the plan changes

Bernalillo County reports that the East Mountains split roughly evenly between homes on wells and homes on community water. And because the limestone aquifer here is fractured, quality shifts from one well to the next. Your filtration plan depends on which side of that split you're on.

Filtration questions from the East Mountains

Will a whole-home filter make my well water safe to drink?

Not by itself. Carbon filtration improves taste, odor and sediment, but it doesn't remove arsenic or nitrate, and it doesn't treat bacteria. Lab testing tells you whether you need reverse osmosis or disinfection too.

I'm on a community system in Sandia Park. Do I need this?

Only if the taste or smell bothers you, or the test turns up something worth addressing. Check your system's annual water quality report and we'll go over it with you.

Does a filter use much extra water?

Filter media is periodically rinsed clean, which does use some water. With groundwater levels down in parts of the mountains, we set that up around your household's actual use and explain it before you decide.
