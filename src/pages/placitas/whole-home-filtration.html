Whole-home filtration for water that comes out of the rock

Every Placitas tap tells a slightly different story. One household on a private well notices a faint sulfur smell in the hot water. Another, on a co-op line, picks up a chlorine note in the shower. A third, sharing a cluster well with neighbors, keeps cleaning fine grit out of faucet aerators. All three call it "something in the water," and all three need different answers.

That variety comes from the ground. Placitas groundwater flows through fractured rock in the Sandia foothills, so what a well delivers depends on which fractures it taps. Add in co-op systems with their own sources and treatment, like the Las Acequias de Placitas system drawing spring water from the Sandias, and you can see why we test before we recommend anything.

Carbon has limits

Catalytic carbon reduces chlorine taste and odor, sediment and many VOCs. Arsenic, nitrate and hardness are outside what carbon can do. If your concern is arsenic, which occurs naturally in groundwater in parts of the region, the answer is reverse osmosis at the kitchen sink, not a whole-home filter.

What we test for at your Placitas tap

The free on-site test runs about 15 minutes, with real-time results and a printed report in plain English. Here's what it covers and why each item matters for filtration choices:

  • Hardness
  • Total dissolved solids
  • pH
  • Chlorine
  • Iron and manganese
  • Sulfur

If you're on a private or shared well, we'll also help you arrange lab testing for bacteria, nitrate, arsenic and more. If bacteria turns up, that calls for disinfection, and we'll refer you rather than sell you a filter.

Placitas filtration FAQs

Our co-op water tastes like chlorine. Will a whole-home filter help?

Yes. If your co-op disinfects with chlorine, catalytic carbon reduces the chlorine taste and odor throughout the house.

Will a whole-home filter make my Placitas well water safe to drink?

A filter improves taste, odor and sediment, but it doesn't remove arsenic, nitrate or bacteria. A lab test tells you what's there. Arsenic and nitrate are handled by RO at the sink, and bacteria needs disinfection.

Does filtration waste a lot of water?

It depends on the media. We'll explain the water use of whatever we recommend for your home, so you can weigh it against what your well or co-op supply can spare.

We share a well with two neighbors. Do they need filtration too?

No. Each home can choose its own treatment, and we install on your branch only.
