Which kind of Placitas water system are you on?

There's no single Placitas water supply. Homes off NM 165, in the older village and out toward the newer subdivisions get their water in different ways, and that shapes everything else.

🏠

Your own private well

You're the water system. Private wells aren't regulated like public ones, so testing is up to you, and we help arrange lab work for bacteria, nitrate, arsenic and more.

🤝

A shared or cluster well

A few households split one well. Everyone gets the same raw water, but each home decides on its own treatment. It's worth knowing who handles testing and what the last results showed.

💧

A community water co-op

Small co-ops serve parts of Placitas. The Anasazi Trails Water Cooperative treats its wells for naturally occurring arsenic, and the Las Acequias de Placitas system draws spring water from the Sandias. Your co-op's annual water quality report is the place for official numbers.

Why no two Placitas wells are alike

Groundwater here moves through fractured rock rather than a broad, uniform sand-and-gravel aquifer like the one beneath the river valley. Water follows cracks and fractures, so a well on one lot can differ in yield and mineral content from a well a short walk away. That's the main reason we don't make assumptions about Placitas water from a zip code.

It also explains why water is precious here. Well owners keep an eye on production, conservation is a household habit, and nobody wants a treatment system that dumps water down the drain for no reason. Efficiency is a design requirement on every system we recommend in Placitas, not an afterthought.

Placitas water questions

I'm on a co-op in Placitas. Do I still need a water test?

Your co-op tests and reports on its water, and its annual report is the place for official numbers. Our free on-site test looks at what matters for comfort and appliances, like hardness and TDS, at your own tap.

Will a water treatment system use a lot of my well's water?

It depends on the system. Softeners use water when they regenerate, and ours only regenerate when metered use calls for it. RO sends some water to drain while it makes drinking water. We'll go over it for your household before you commit.

Our cluster well serves four homes. Can one house add a softener?

Yes. Treatment goes on your own line after the water reaches your home, so you can soften or filter your house without changing anything for the neighbors.

Is arsenic a concern in Placitas?

Arsenic occurs naturally in groundwater in parts of the Middle Rio Grande region, and at least one Placitas co-op treats for it. On a private or shared well, a lab test is the only way to know your level, and we can help arrange one.
